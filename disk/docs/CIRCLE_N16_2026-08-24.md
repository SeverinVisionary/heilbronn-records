# n = 16 in the unit-radius disk: Cantrell's row reproduced, not beaten

```
alpha_disk(16) >= 33067382155420434262092576074846779688379
                  / 500000000000000000000000000000000000000000
                = 0.066134764310840866...
```

Friedman's page, verbatim:

> **16. A = .0661+ Found by David Cantrell, August 2006. Symmetry of a square.**

Our value lies in `[.0661, .0662)`, so it satisfies that row. The agreement is
in fact exact to every published digit: transporting our value back to
MathWorld's unit-AREA normalization gives

```
H_16 = 0.066134764310840866 / pi = 0.021051349301...
```

against MathWorld's printed `H_16 = 0.021051...`. Every digit Wolfram prints is
reproduced. Transporting the other way, `pi * 0.021051 = 0.066133666951`, the
margin is **+0.0017%** — far below the ~0.05% bar, and Friedman prints three
significant figures, which cannot discriminate anything at this scale.

(An earlier draft of this note put the baseline at `0.066127749` and the margin
at `+0.0106%`. That was an arithmetic slip: `0.021051 * pi = 0.066133667`. The
verdict is unchanged, and the corrected figure makes the reproduction *tighter*,
not looser.)

**Verdict: matches the published value.** This is a reproduction of Cantrell's
construction, not a record. Given that the 2026-08-23 census reproduced him at
seven of nine rows, this is the outcome the evidence pointed to.

## The configuration

8 points on the bounding circle, 4 interior at radius `0.985538965`, 4 interior
at radius `0.383820444`. Measured on the symmetry-constrained optimum:
reflection defect **exactly 0**, `C_4` defect `5.6e-17` — it *is* the
square-symmetric configuration Friedman annotates. 28 of the 560 triangles are
active.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| exact minimum | `33067382155420434262092576074846779688379 / 5*10^41` |
| float | `0.066134764310840866` |
| grid | `1/10^21`, exact containment verified in integer arithmetic |
| containment slack | `1.3e20` in `scale^2` units — strictly inside |
| boundary + interior | **8 + 8** |
| active triangles | 28 of 560 |
| exact rational ties | 1 (see the n=14 note: intrinsic, not a snap artifact) |
| reflection defect | 0 (constrained optimum) / `3.0e-06` (after free ascent) |

## Random multistart does not find this row

The deep unrestricted sweep was run to **6912 restarts x 600k SA iterations**
(boundary count cycled over 3..16 across restarts, sequential-LP endgame on the
top 1800 candidates by pre-LP value; the next 900 came back at 0.0533, so the
pre-LP ordering is a sound filter). Its best:

```
0.059614213620487293551031 / 10^24   =  0.059614213620
6 on the circle + 10 interior, 25 of 560 active, ASYMMETRIC (reflection defect 1.1e-01)
```

That is **9.86% below** the constructed value. The previous, independent
campaign's unrestricted passes reached `0.059832796747` — **9.5% below** — so
two deep sweeps agree with each other and both miss the row by a tenth.

By final boundary/interior split, over the 1800 polished candidates:

| split | best | candidates ending there |
|---|---|---|
| 3+13 | 0.053825742728 | 4 |
| 4+12 | 0.054821169582 | 38 |
| 5+11 | 0.057773397240 | 368 |
| **6+10** | **0.059614213621** | 685 |
| 7+9 | 0.056427219502 | 496 |
| 8+8 | 0.056753793489 | 162 |
| 9+7 | 0.052738133877 | 35 |
| 10+6 | 0.052023666933 | 7 |
| 13+3 | 0.053231239097 | 5 |

Note where the annealer *wants* to go: 6+10, not the 8+8 of the true optimum.
162 candidates did land in 8+8 and the best of them still only reaches 0.0568.
This is the same failure mode as the naive n = 11 multistart that stalled 22%
low, and the reason the 2026-08-23 prior-art note warns that "a solver failing
to reach a value from random starts proves nothing about whether the value is
attainable".

Constructing the symmetry class directly reaches it in **120 seeds**. That is
the methodological point of this row: at n = 16 the search had to be told where
to look, and the place to look was the class Friedman had already annotated.

## Asymmetry buys nothing here — three independent tests

At n = 13 and n = 14 the winning configurations are asymmetric, and that was the
one surviving lead. It does not extend to n = 16.

**1. Every boundary/interior split, k = 8..16.** Each family polished with the
split enforced (boundary points held on the circle by a tangential-displacement
equality), then with the constraint released:

| k | family | in-family | released |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8** | **8+8** | **0.066134764310** | 0.066134764311 (stays 8+8) |
| 9 | 9+7 | 0.065730429156 | 0.066134764311 (flows to 8+8) |
| 10 | 10+6 | 0.065410099022 | 0.066134764311 (flows to 8+8) |
| 11 | 11+5 | 0.065032683792 | 0.066134764311 (flows to 8+8) |
| 12 | 12+4 | 0.064808790547 | 0.066134764311 (flows to 8+8) |
| 13 | 13+3 | 0.050936777380 | 0.052198178447 |
| 14 | 14+2 | 0.042967997790 | 0.042967997883 (stays) |
| 15 | 15+1 | 0.035164230337 | 0.035164230337 (stays) |
| 16 | 16+0 | 0.029130041772 | 0.029130041772 (the regular 16-gon) |

Monotone away from k = 8, and every family from 9 to 12 flows straight back to
8+8 once released. 8+8 is the attractor, not one option among several.

*Seeding caveat that makes these numbers meaningful:* cold random seeds are
useless at this row — 240 of them reach only `0.041072598113` in the b = 8
family. Every family above is entered from the best configuration found
anywhere, pushed into the family, with annealed candidates and random seeds as
controls.

**2. Asymmetric kicks around the D_4 optimum**, 40 trials at each of eight
sigmas:

| sigma | best after free polish | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0.002 – 0.04 | `0.0661347643110` | **40/40 trials return to the same value** |
| 0.08 | 0.046937059897 | falls out of the basin |
| 0.15 | 0.042228182727 | falls out of the basin |
| 0.30 | 0.034409518069 | falls out of the basin |

Differences inside the returning band are `~1e-13`, i.e. LP round-off; the
exact snapped value of the best kicked configuration comes back marginally
*lower*. The winners' measured reflection defects (`1e-6`..`6e-5`) are drift,
not structure.

**3. Relaxing the symmetry from Cantrell's own optimum.** The per-class table
from random seeding cannot answer this question, because `C_4`, `C_2`, `D_2`
and the reflection class all *contain* the `D_4` configurations — a random seed
inside them measures the seeding, not the class. So each subgroup's permutation
is read off the configuration by matching the rotated/reflected point set back
onto it (match residuals `5.6e-17`, `1.6e-16`, `0.0` — the generators are
exact), and the polish is restarted from the `D_4` point plus within-class
perturbations at `sigma = 0.005 / 0.02 / 0.06`:

| constraint | result |
|---|---|
| `D_4` (as found) | 0.066134764310736 |
| `C_4` — mirror dropped | 0.066134764310498 |
| `C_2` — half turn only | 0.066134764310557 |
| mirror only | 0.066134764310736 |
| unrestricted | 0.066134764310746 |

Spread `2.5e-13` absolute. Dropping the mirror, dropping the quarter turn, or
dropping every constraint all leave the configuration exactly where it is.

## Budget

Machine: 10 logical cores (8 performance + 2 efficiency), **shared with other
sessions** — measured load average 15–42 throughout, and the process only ever
received 0.9–6.5 cores foreground, ~0.5 cores once a job was backgrounded.
Unrestricted sweep: 6912 restarts x 600k, ~4900 CPU-seconds of annealing over
about 2 h 15 m of wall time, plus 1800 sequential-LP polishes. Two scheduling facts cost most of the wall time and are
worth recording:

* a child exec'd from Python's `subprocess` inherits a background QoS on this
  host — the identical 64-restart annealer call takes **6.8 s** launched by the
  shell and **over 110 s** launched by `subprocess.run`. `circle_pipeline.py`
  drives the annealer the slow way. `sweep_chunk.sh` / `absorb_chunk.py` split
  it so the shell launches the annealer and Python only absorbs its output.
* an orphaned `heil 16 600000 32768 10 ...` from the previous, crashed campaign
  had been running for **12 h 48 m** with no reachable stdout — its entire
  output was going to be discarded. It was consuming a core throughout. Killed.

## Caveats

This is a **CONSTRUCTION**: an exactly certified lower bound on
`alpha_disk(16)`. It is not a proof of optimality, and no optimality was
claimed for the published row either. Floating point proposes; the reported
number is an exact rational recomputed from integer-scaled coordinates whose
containment in the closed unit disk is checked in integer arithmetic, and
re-verified by `verify_circle_config.py`, which imports nothing from the
producing pipeline.


## Downgraded after the 2026-08-24 review panel

All three reviewers independently raised the same objection, so it is recorded
here rather than argued with: **the three asymmetry tests are not independent.**

`n16_splits.py` enters every family from the best configuration found anywhere
(the D_4 optimum); `n16_break.py` and `n16_relax.py` both load a single anchor
and add Gaussian kicks, with the largest sigma explored still inside the basin
(past 0.08 the value collapses to 0.047, so the kicks measure basin *width* and
never reach a second basin). All three are local sequential-LP polishing seeded
from the same point, and the split experiment systematically covers only
`k = 8..16`.

What makes this matter is our own evidence: the unrestricted sweep
(6912 x 600k) tops out 9.86% below the D_4 row, so **the pipeline has
demonstrated zero ability to find any n=16 optimum it was not told about**. An
asymmetric basin separated by a low saddle — exactly the situation at n=14 —
would evade every one of the three tests, together, for the same reason.

This campaign's own rule was written for the opposite direction: *a solver
failing to reach a value from random starts proves nothing.* It applies here in
the mirror.

**The claim "asymmetry buys nothing at n=16" is therefore downgraded from
*refuted* to *no evidence for, at this one row*.** Closing it properly needs a
search seeded from configurations that are not descendants of D_4 — push the
best 6+10 unrestricted result into 8+8 and polish — plus systematic coverage of
`k = 3..7` and of the C_3/C_5/D_2 classes constructed directly, as D_4 was.

None of this touches the n=14 record, which is a *positive* certified bound.
