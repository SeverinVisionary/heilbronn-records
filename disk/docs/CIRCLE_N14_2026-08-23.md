## What is NOT claimed

- **Local optimality is not claimed, as a theorem or otherwise.** The
  configuration is now a *numerically* stationary candidate — the evidence is in
  [Convergence](#convergence-why-we-stopped) and in
  `circle_configs/n14_stationarity.json` — but numerical stationarity is
  context, not proof. The rigorous statement is the exact rational lower bound
  and nothing more. The true `alpha_disk(14)` may still be higher.
- **The exact tie count is a snap artifact and says nothing about convergence.**
  At the stationary point all 16 active triangles are equal to `1.4e-44`
  relative; after snapping to the `1/10^33` grid exactly one attains the integer
  minimum and 16 sit within one grid step. Earlier drafts read the single tie as
  evidence first for and then against convergence. Both readings were wrong: it
  is a property of the grid, not of the configuration.
- **"Cantrell searched only symmetry classes" is an inference, not a fact.**
  Friedman annotates a symmetry class for every row (n=14 is "Horizontally
  symmetric"), and our winner is asymmetric. That is suggestive, but the caption
  describes the *displayed configuration*, not the *search method*. Cantrell may
  have searched freely and found a symmetric optimum. Settling it needs the
  original correspondence, which we do not have.

## Convergence: why we stopped

A referee asked the obvious question about the previous deposit: a feasible
ascent direction was known to exist at the certified point, so why publish a
value we already knew to be improvable? The answer turned out to be structural,
and it retires the word "converged" from every earlier pass.

**The equality set is a curve, not a point.** With 16 active triangles, 8
boundary points carrying one tangential degree of freedom each, 4 interior
active points carrying two each, and 2 points in no active triangle, the system
`sigma_T area_T = t` has 16 equations in 16 spatial degrees of freedom — and its
Jacobian has rank **15**, because rotating the whole configuration changes
nothing. So `{all 16 active triangles equal}` is one-dimensional, and `t` varies
along it. Every earlier pass ran a Newton solve, landed *somewhere* on that
curve, saw a residual of `1e-121` and called it converged. That is exactly how a
feasible ascent direction survives a convergence label.

[n14_converge.py](n14_converge.py) walks the curve: predictor along the solution
of `J v = 1` (the direction that raises every active triangle at the same rate),
corrector back onto the curve by a least-squares Newton in the tangency
parameterisation, one-dimensional maximisation of `t`, repeated until the gain
dies. Per-iteration gains: `8.1e-18`, `8.7e-29`, `9.6e-40`, `1.1e-50`, …,
`1.6e-94`.

**Two independent stopping certificates**, both committed as data in
[circle_configs/n14_stationarity.json](circle_configs/n14_stationarity.json):

| quantity | Newton point | after the walk |
|---|---|---|
| `J v = 1` residual — *consistent means an ascent direction exists* | `5.5e-106` — solvable | `5.1e-17` and climbing — **inconsistent**, no direction raises every active triangle |
| best equal-rate ascent rate, per unit displacement | `1.25e-9` | `1.9e-53` |
| KKT residual for `sum lambda_T grad(sigma_T area_T) = sum 2 mu_i p_i`, `sum lambda = 1` | `3.5e-10` — **no multipliers exist** | `5.3e-54` |
| smallest multiplier | — | `min lambda = 1.10e-2`, `min mu = 1.38e-3`, both **strictly positive** |

The middle row is the least tolerance-sensitive way to read the result: the
remaining ascent rate falls by 44 orders of magnitude, from something a
`1e-8`-sized step could exploit to something no representable step can.

The multipliers are a one-parameter family (rank 23 of 24 unknowns), so the
member reported is the one maximising the smallest multiplier — the strongest
available, not an arbitrary pick by the solver. Strict complementarity means no
active constraint is being carried for free.

**What it bought: the 15th significant digit.**

| | value |
|---|---|
| previous certified | `0.0767158857710272457569510867362245565` |
| stationary, high precision | `0.076715885771028939751784775506634…` |
| **certified after snapping to `1/10^33`** | **`0.076715885771028939751784775506633485800988522351575884799934177551`** |

The gain is `+1.694e-15`, `+2.208e-14` relative. Against Friedman's printed
`.0758+` the margin moves from `+1.075%` to `+1.074948%` — unchanged at any
precision anyone quotes. **That smallness is the answer to "why did you stop?":
the earlier point was already within `2.2e-14` relative of the top of its curve.
What was missing was not compute but the evidence.**

The certified rational sits `7.6e-34` below the high-precision stationary value.
That gap is what exactness costs, and the exact one is the claim.

**Two caveats recorded rather than smoothed over.** Points 4 and 9 sit in no
active triangle, so the stationary point is *not isolated* — they move freely
within their slack region without changing the minimum. And re-running the walk
on its own output reproduces the same stationary value but re-snaps to a
neighbouring grid point, `1.9e-34` away: the deposit is reproducible to about
one unit in the last place of the `10^33` grid, no finer. The claim rests on the
committed integers, not on the 45-digit `points_hp` field.

## Method

Multistart simulated annealing (C, pthreads, boundary count cycled over `3..n`)
-> sequential-LP polish (HiGHS) -> high-precision Newton onto the active-set
curve -> walk that curve to the maximum of `t` -> snap to a `1/scale` integer
grid -> exact rational minimum with an exact closed-disk containment check.
Floating point proposes; rational arithmetic decides.
